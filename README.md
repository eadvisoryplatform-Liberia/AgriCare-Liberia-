AgriCare Liberia
Inclusive Agricultural e-Advisory Platform
Empowering Farmers. Improving Livelihoods. Growing Liberia.

AgriCare Liberia is a digital agricultural advisory platform designed to
make practical, timely, and localized agricultural information
accessible to smallholder farmers across Liberia. It brings together
AI-assisted advisory services, local agricultural knowledge, extension
support, weather information, food-security monitoring, awareness
resources, and a farmer-focused marketplace.

The platform is designed around the realities of rural communities:
varying literacy levels, diverse local languages, limited internet
connectivity, and the widespread use of basic mobile phones.
Our mission: Ensure every farmer can access the right agricultural
knowledge at the right time, regardless of literacy level, language,
gender, location, or device type.

The Challenge
Smallholder farmers play a vital role in Liberia's food systems and
rural livelihoods. However, many farmers have limited access to reliable
and timely information that can help them make sound production and
marketing decisions.

Common challenges include:
- Pest and crop disease outbreaks without timely diagnostic support.
- Limited access to improved seeds, inputs, and practical farming
  techniques.
- Unpredictable rainfall and changing climate conditions.
- Inadequate access to agricultural extension officers and
  specialists.
- Limited visibility into market prices, buyers, and selling
  opportunities.
- Language, literacy, connectivity, and device barriers that restrict
  access to digital services.
Agricultural information is most useful when it is understandable,
locally relevant, trustworthy, and available through channels farmers
can use.
Our Solution
AgriCare Liberia connects farmers with agricultural information and
support through a unified, mobile-friendly platform. It combines digital
tools with local knowledge and human expertise to help farmers make more
informed decisions throughout the agricultural cycle.

The platform is intended to support:
- Crop-specific production recommendations.
- Weather and climate information for farm planning.
- Pest and disease identification and management guidance.
- Food-security information and awareness resources.
- Agricultural input and produce discovery through a marketplace.
- Communication between farmers, extension workers, and subject-matter
  experts.
- Local-language and voice-enabled access as these capabilities are
  implemented.
- Low-bandwidth and offline-friendly experiences where technically
  supported.
Platform Modules
1. Dashboard
The dashboard provides a central overview of agricultural information
and platform activity. It is organized around three key areas:
Food Security Dashboard
Provides an overview of food-security conditions and relevant
indicators. Depending on available and validated data, it can present:
- Food availability and access indicators.
- Crop production and harvest information.
- Food-price trends and market signals.
- Local alerts and emerging food-security concerns.
- Summaries by county, district, or community where data is available.
The dashboard should clearly display data sources, reporting dates,
geographic coverage, and any limitations. It should not present
estimates as verified facts.
Awareness Dashboard
Provides farmers and communities with accessible educational and
public-awareness content, such as:
- Good agricultural practices.
- Climate-smart agriculture and environmental protection.
- Pest and disease prevention.
- Nutrition and food-safety information.
- Seasonal farming campaigns and public notices.
- Training materials, announcements, and community alerts.
Content can be organized by topic, crop, season, location, and intended
audience.
Marketplace Dashboard
Provides an overview of buying and selling activity across the
agricultural marketplace, including:
- Available agricultural products and inputs.
- Farmer produce listings.
- Indicative market prices, where verified.
- Buyer and seller activity.
- New listings and relevant market opportunities.
Listings, prices, and seller information should include timestamps and
verification status where applicable.
2. Weather and Climate Advisory
- Localized weather information.
- Rainfall outlooks and seasonal forecasts.
- Planting and harvesting guidance informed by weather conditions.
- Early-warning messages for relevant climate risks.
3. Smart Crop Advisory
- Crop selection and suitability guidance.
- Planting calendars and spacing recommendations.
- Soil-fertility and fertilizer guidance.
- Irrigation and water-management practices.
- Harvest and post-harvest recommendations.
Recommendations should account for location, crop, season, and locally
validated agricultural guidance.
4. Pest and Disease Support
- Guided pest and disease reporting.
- Image-assisted identification where supported.
- Symptoms, prevention, and management information.
- Escalation to an extension worker or qualified expert when a
  diagnosis is uncertain.
AI-generated suggestions are advisory and should not replace
professional diagnosis, especially where incorrect treatment could cause
harm.
5. Agricultural Marketplace
The marketplace connects farmers, buyers, and agricultural input
providers. Potential capabilities include:
- Produce and input listings.
- Product categories, quantities, and locations.
- Seller and buyer contact or inquiry options.
- Price information and listing dates.
- Search and filtering by product and location.
- Listing moderation and reporting mechanisms.
The marketplace should make clear that displayed prices and availability
may change and should be confirmed directly with the seller.
6. Voice-Based Advisory
Designed to improve access for people who prefer spoken information or
face literacy barriers:
- Interactive Voice Response (IVR).
- Recorded agricultural tips.
- Voice prompts and navigation.
- Local-language audio content as translations become available.
7. Multi-Channel Access
The platform is designed to support multiple access channels over time:
- Responsive web application.
- Progressive Web Application (PWA).
- Mobile application, if required.
- SMS notifications.
- USSD services.
- Voice hotline and IVR integration.
Availability of each channel depends on implementation and integration
with relevant service providers.
8. Farmer and Expert Engagement
- Farmer questions and advisory requests.
- Extension-worker and expert consultation.
- Community knowledge exchange.
- Follow-up and referral workflows.
- Feedback on the usefulness of advice.
Inclusive Access
Inclusion is a core design requirement, not an optional feature.
AgriCare Liberia aims to:
- Support clear, simple language and accessible interfaces.
- Provide local-language content, including languages such as Kpelle,
  Bassa, Gio, Mano, and Grebo as validated translations become
  available.
- Offer audio and voice-based experiences.
- Design for low-bandwidth connections and small screens.
- Make key information usable on basic mobile devices through SMS and
  USSD integrations.
- Consider accessibility needs of people with disabilities and older
  users.
- Include women, young people, remote communities, and
  first-generation farmers in design and testing.
Translations and agricultural recommendations should be reviewed by
fluent speakers and qualified local agricultural practitioners before
publication.
Who We Serve
- Smallholder and subsistence farmers.
- Women farmers and farmer groups.
- Youth engaged in agriculture.
- Farmers in remote and underserved communities.
- Agricultural cooperatives and producer organizations.
- Extension officers and agricultural experts.
- Produce buyers, traders, and agricultural input suppliers.
- Development organizations and agricultural institutions.
Expected Outcomes
AgriCare Liberia aims to contribute to:
- Improved access to relevant agricultural knowledge.
- Better-informed planting, production, and marketing decisions.
- Earlier awareness of pest, disease, and climate risks.
- Reduced avoidable crop losses.
- Improved market visibility and opportunities.
- Stronger connections between farmers and extension services.
- Greater inclusion in agricultural information systems.
- Improved resilience and food security.
These are intended outcomes. Actual impact should be assessed through
baseline data, monitoring, and independent evaluation rather than
assumed.
